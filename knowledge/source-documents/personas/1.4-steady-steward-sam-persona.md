# Master Ideal Customer Persona: Steady Steward Sam

## 1. Executive Summary

Steady Steward Sam is an established rural or acreage homeowner in Bragg Creek, Rocky View County, Bearspaw, or Water Valley who wants careful property improvements without the stress of managing multiple trades, uncertain quality, or unfamiliar people moving through their home. Sam is the ideal customer because Haven Creek Renovations’ strongest fit is not generic renovation work; it is trusted, hands-on, phased renovation support for rural homeowners who value privacy, continuity, craftsmanship, and long-term relationship value.

## 2. Demographics  Demographics & Firmographics

- **Role/Title:** Rural homeowner, acreage owner, family property steward, or primary home-improvement decision-maker
- **Seniority Level:** Primary or shared household decision-maker; often consults a spouse, partner, or family before larger property investments
- **Industry:** Rural residential ownership, acreage living, family estate/property stewardship, small business ownership, professional services, agriculture-adjacent, semi-retired, or retired
- **Company Size (Revenue/Employees):** Not typically a company buyer; personally held rural home or acreage with meaningful long-term property value
- **Age Range:** 45–70
- **Location:** Bragg Creek, Rocky View County, Bearspaw, Water Valley, and surrounding rural Calgary-area communities
- **Key Tools They Use Daily:** Google Search, Google Maps, Google Business Profiles, contractor websites, project galleries, email, phone calls, local referrals, community Facebook groups, neighbour recommendations, municipal/property information, Pinterest or Houzz for inspiration, and saved photos of work they like

## 3. The Narrative: A Day in Their Life

Sam starts the morning with coffee near a window that overlooks the property. The land is the reason they live here: the trees, privacy, quiet, space, and feeling of distance from the city. But with that space comes an endless mental list. The deck needs attention before another season passes. The exterior has a few problem areas that should not be ignored. Inside, there are finishing details that have never felt fully complete.

Sam is not impulsive. They have hired contractors before, and they know the difference between someone who simply “does the job” and someone who respects the property. During the day, they may search for renovation contractors in Bragg Creek or Rocky View County, but they are not just comparing services. They are reading tone. They are looking at project photos. They are asking: does this company feel grounded, careful, and compatible?

By evening, Sam is thinking less about one isolated project and more about a longer-term plan. They do not want to explain the property to a new contractor every time something needs attention. They do not want a rotating cast of trades coming and going. They want one reliable person who can understand the home, remember the context, see the work through properly, and return for the next phase when the time is right. What keeps Sam up at night is not just cost. It is the fear of choosing the wrong person and feeling exposed in their own home.

## 4. The Core "Job to Be Done" (JTBD)

*   **Functional Job:** Sam needs to complete interior finishing, exterior repairs, decking, or phased property improvements with one trusted contractor who can plan, coordinate, execute, and finish the work properly.
*   **Social Job:** Sam wants to be seen as a discerning, responsible property owner who takes care of the home and land with good judgment, not someone who hires the cheapest or fastest option.
*   **Emotional Job:** Sam wants to feel confident, safe, respected, relieved, and in control of the renovation process. The deeper emotional job is: “Help me improve my property without feeling like I have handed my home over to strangers.”

## 5. Pains & Frustrations (The "Before" State)

*   **Quote:** “Will this person respect my property?” Sam’s first concern is whether the contractor will treat the home, land, privacy, and family environment with care.
*   **Quote:** “Can I trust them around my home?” The decision feels personal because rural work often happens in private spaces, not anonymous urban job sites.
*   **Quote:** “Will I be dealing with too many people?” Sam does not want a fragmented process with disconnected trades, unclear accountability, or too many unfamiliar workers coming and going.
*   **Quote:** “Will the project be handled properly?” Sam worries about sloppy details, unfinished edges, poor communication, and work that technically gets done but does not feel complete.
*   **Quote:** “Can this contractor support future projects too?” Sam is not only buying a single renovation. They are looking for a long-term property relationship, especially for phased improvements over time.
*   Sam feels underserved by large renovation firms that seem too big, broad contractors that feel transactional, and service menus that promise everything but do not communicate trust.
*   Sam dislikes high-pressure quote language, cheap-sounding CTAs, and websites that feel flashy, confusing, urban, or generic.

## 6. Goals & Aspirations (The "After" State)

*   **Professional/Practical Goal:** To complete high-quality property improvements that protect, maintain, and enhance the home’s long-term value.
*   **Property Goal:** To bring the home closer to the way it should feel: finished, durable, comfortable, cared for, and aligned with rural living.
*   **Relationship Goal:** To find one contractor they can call again for the next phase, instead of restarting the trust-building process every time.
*   **Lifestyle Goal:** To enjoy the property more fully through better outdoor living, repaired exterior details, and interior spaces that feel complete.

- **Emotional Goal:** To move from uncertainty to confidence: “I know who is doing the work, I know they understand the property, and I trust them to see it through.”
- **Personal Goal:** To reduce the mental burden of unfinished projects and free up energy for family, land, hobbies, travel, hosting, or simply enjoying the acreage.

## 7. Watering Holes & Influences
- **Information Sources:** Google Search, Google Maps, Google Business Profiles, contractor websites, service-area pages, project galleries, local renovation examples, municipal/property guidance, and word-of-mouth referrals. Local search behavior should be supported with pages for Bragg Creek, Rocky View County, Bearspaw, and Water Valley.
- **Social Media:** Facebook community groups, local rural/resident groups, Instagram for visual proof, Pinterest or Houzz for inspiration, and occasional YouTube searches for project ideas or construction quality cues.
- **Influencers/Thought Leaders:** Neighbours, other acreage owners, previous clients, local tradespeople, suppliers, family members, and trusted community voices matter more than broad design influencers.
- **Decision-Making Process:** Sam searches locally, reviews photos first, studies the tone of the website, looks for signs of real work and property respect, checks whether the company serves their area, compares the contractor’s focus against the scope of their project, then reaches out only after the company feels safe and credible. The project gallery becomes one of the most important trust assets because it turns past results into future confidence.

## 8. Objections jections & Barriers to Purchase
- **Trust:** “How do I know this person is safe, respectful, and compatible with how I want the property handled?”
- **Budget:** “Is this worth the cost compared to the other projects on my property list?”
- **Complexity/Implementation:** “Will this become a bigger coordination headache than I expected?”
- **Proof:** “Have they done work that looks like what I need, in rural homes like mine?”
- **Fit:** “Are they too small for the job, too big for the relationship, or too broad to care about the details?”
- **Timing:** “Should I do this now, or wait until I can bundle it with the next phase?”
- **Quote Anxiety:** Because pricing varies by scope, site conditions, materials, and sequencing, Sam needs the quote process to feel thoughtful rather than evasive.

## 9. LANDING PAGE HANDOFF BRIEF
- **Mindset Upon Arrival:** Sam arrives cautious, selective, and quietly skeptical. They may be actively looking for a contractor, but they are not ready to be sold aggressively. Their emotional state is: “I need help, but I need to know I can trust you first.” The page should create calm confidence within the first few seconds, not urgency.
- **Key Question to Answer in 5 Seconds:** “Can I trust this contractor to respect my rural property, do quality work, and see the project through properly?”
- **Primary Hook:** “One trusted contractor for the property you value.” This speaks directly to Sam’s desire for trust, continuity, property respect, and long-term relationship value.
